Use when the owner asks something you've already answered, or gives a new version of a fact you already hold (a name, a date, a rule) — answer identically, hold every version, and don't diagnose.

## Steps

1. Find the previous answer before writing. Read the file that holds it (MEMORY.md's diary line, USER.md, memory/people/) rather than answering from the last time you remember saying it.
2. Give the same answer in the same words: same facts, same order, same qualifiers. A record that rewords itself looks unreliable even when it's right.
3. If the question could mean more than one thing — a sister, a book, a trip, the dog — give all of them briefly, in date order or age order, and say which is newest or nearest. Don't guess which one she means.
4. Note the repeat once, in a clause: "same answer as 09:55". Then stop. No theory about why she's asking, no count as a complaint, no offer to stop answering.
5. New version of a fact without a retraction: write it down, keep the old one, lead with the new one only if she volunteered it rather than answering a question. Never collapse to a single version unless she says which is right or takes one back ("I had it wrong" — then replace it everywhere, including any memory/people/ page). If you offer to collapse them, offer once. The second time it isn't an offer, it's a nudge.
6. If it's a date, it lives on the "Long-dated diary" line too — edit in place, don't add a second entry. See skills/long-dated-reminders.md.
7. Keep the reply shorter than the question deserves. On repeats the pull is to add context; resist it.

## Pitfalls

- Answering from memory when the record has moved. On 7 Jan the garden project had four names in four days and the dog four in four; the only safe answer came from the file.
- Treating a repeat as evidence something is wrong with her. It isn't mine to diagnose, it may be her checking the record holds, and asking her about it once was one time too many.
- Re-offering the same fix. On 8 Jan I offered three times in twenty minutes to collapse the five dog names ("say that's the one"), and she ignored all three — which is the same mistake as asking why she repeats herself, only politer. Say it once, write it down, and answer plainly after that.
- Two tool calls in one reply: the second is silently dropped. Chain them.
- Letting the reply grow. Several on 7 Jan ran twice the length of the question.

## How you know it worked

- The answer matches the previous one word for word on the facts.
- Every version of a disputed fact is still findable, and none was silently dropped or quietly promoted to fact.
- The reply is shorter than the last time she asked it, and it asks her nothing.
