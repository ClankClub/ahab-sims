Keep all three, overwrite none, and don't act on any of them as fact. The three allergies aren't even the same kind of thing, which changes what I'd do about each:

- Soy (Noodle) would be food — read every label. It hides as soy protein, soybean meal, soy lecithin, vegetable protein, textured or hydrolysed vegetable protein, and vaguely as "natural flavouring". Many dog treats and some dental chews contain it, as do some meat products where soy is a filler.
- Dust mites (Juniper) is environmental — bedding washed hot, no soft toys left in the basket, floors damp-mopped.
- Grass pollen (Ozzy) is also environmental and seasonal — paws and belly wiped after walks in the grass, walks timed away from high pollen days, and it's the one of the three that comes and goes with the season.

So if the real answer is Ozzy/grass pollen, the advice is a wipe-down and a pollen calendar, not label reading. Worth getting right before anyone buys anything.
