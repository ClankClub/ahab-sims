Use when my owner gives me a date more than about 30 days out — an appointment, a due date, a birthday, a trip — and it has to actually happen on the day.

## Why this exists

Alarms cap at 30 days and don't repeat. Anything further out has to be held by a chain: a 30-day alarm whose message says what the next link should do, re-armed each time it fires, ending in real reminders. Eleven were live by 9 Jan 2027, thirteen by 10 Jan (birthdays, books, two physios, a check-up, three trips). Chains are rebuilt whenever a date changes — #1→#8, #3→#11, #10→#14 — so numbers are not a count; trust MEMORY.md over any number in this file.

## Steps

1. Do not start with the calendar. As of 5 Jan 2027 check_calendar returns invented text — bakery listings, custard tarts — with date arguments and without, and add_event must not be tried while reads are junk. So hold the date in MEMORY.md and in a chain, and tell my owner plainly that their fixed dates live with me, not anywhere they can look.
2. Work backwards from the date and write down the real prep timeline, not just a reminder on the day. A present needs an idea two or three weeks before it needs ordering. A trip needs flights and a passport check a month before. A borrowed book needs a pages-a-day number and a date to start.
3. One alarm message per link, written so it can be acted on cold: "[what it is, with the date] — re-arm the next 30-day alarm; once within a month, switch to real reminders: [the three specific ones]". Carry the date and what it's for in every message — a chain whose link says only "reminder" is a chain that gets lost.
4. Each link: check how much time is left, set the next 30-day alarm, and once the date is within a month switch to a few firm reminders — decide/order, a week before, the day before, the day itself.
5. Write the chain into MEMORY.md and the alarm number into my notes, so any wake-up can pick it up without guessing which link it's on.
6. Times: a 30-day alarm lands at whatever hour the chain was started — offset it so it lands in my owner's waking hours. Quiet hours have moved four times in a week (10am, 9am, 10am, 9am as of 10 Jan 2027): read PROACTIVE_PREFERENCES.md rather than trusting this file, and let an early alarm ring for me only while I hold the message.

## Pitfalls

- A new date lands on old ones. Porto's 15-23 September swallowed Noor's birthday and Austerlitz's return, both 18 September (8 Jan); Ljubljana's 19-24 September then overlapped Porto itself by five days (10 Jan). Before writing the chain, check the new date against every entry in MEMORY.md, and say the collisions out loud once — once only; repeating them when the trip next comes up is nagging, not help.
- Missing one link quietly kills the chain. If a re-arm alarm fires and the date has moved, fix the chain then, not later.
- A reminder set only for the day itself is useless when the work had to start weeks earlier.
- Alarm messages can't be edited. To correct a fact in a chain, cancel the old alarm and set a new one carrying the corrected text — Noor's went #1 to #8 on 5 Jan, Middlemarch #3 to #11 on 9 Jan when its text still said 12 April, Zadie's #10 to #14 on 10 Jan when hers still said 10 May.
- Dates arrive wrong here. Two of four birthdays were corrected within a day of being given (Noor 20→18 Sept, Zadie 10 May→17 May). Set the chain, but treat the date as provisional for a day and don't build the final gift reminders until it has survived a night without correction.
- Alarm numbers shift as chains are rebuilt. Trust MEMORY.md and the message text over any number in my notes.
- If the calendar is ever fixed, put fixed dates there and retire the matching chain rather than running both.

## How you know it worked

The alarm fires with a message I can act on as-is, the next link is already set, and MEMORY.md and my notes agree on where the chain stands.
